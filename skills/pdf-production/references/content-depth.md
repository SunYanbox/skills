# Content Depth Standards

Applies to any document generated from scratch (Report and Academic lines). The goal is
to avoid "shallow" documents — headings followed by one or two sentences, bullet lists
with no explanation, conclusions that only repeat the introduction.

## Minimum standards

- **Paragraphs**: aim for 3–5 sentences each. A paragraph should develop one complete
  idea with supporting detail. Single-sentence paragraphs are allowed only as deliberate
  transitions.
- **Sections**: a heading must be followed by substantial content. If a section cannot
  reach roughly 150–200 words (Chinese: about 100–150 characters of body text is too
  thin; aim higher), merge it with a related section instead of leaving it starved.
- **Bullet lists**: every list needs an introductory sentence that frames it, and each
  bullet should carry enough context to stand alone. A bare list with no lead-in reads
  as scaffolding, not writing.
- **Conclusions**: must add something — a judgment, a recommendation, a synthesis.
  Restating the introduction verbatim is forbidden.

## Enrichment techniques

When a section feels thin, add substance rather than padding:

- Give concrete numbers, examples, or a short case.
- Explain *why* and *how*, not just *what*.
- Add context: history, current state, trend direction.
- Surface a second perspective, a limitation, or a counter-argument.
- Translate an abstract point into a concrete scenario.

For analysis/report content specifically:

- Compare alternatives and say which is better under what conditions.
- Attach a measurable outcome to each recommendation.
- State assumptions and the risk if they don't hold.

For explanatory content:

- Use an analogy to anchor a hard concept.
- Break a process into numbered steps.
- Mention a common misconception and correct it.

## Anti-patterns

- Heading followed by 1–3 bare sentences.
- A list that only repeats the heading in itemized form.
- Filler sentences that restate what was just said.
- Sections pointing backward ("as mentioned above") without adding new information.
- A conclusion that summarizes the introduction instead of answering "so what?"
